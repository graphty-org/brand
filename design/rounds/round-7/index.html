graphty brand color, round 7: bright green, pink, and the app

Labels to reply with: the mark on white (G-W1G-W2G-W3G-W4), the brand palette (PK-APK-BPK-C for pink, GR-BGR-C for green). GR-A is the bright green dropped straight into the app, shown so you can see why it cannot stay that way. The circle (N1, N2, N3) is still open; everything here uses N1.

Recommendations.

  • Pink: PK-A. Magenta can be the app's own accent as it is: white text on a magenta button passes (4.5:1), and in the app it only lands on small things (the Run and Done buttons, the active tool, links), where it reads as lively rather than loud.
  • Green: GR-B. Bright lime works as the app accent only with dark text on it (14.5:1). White text on lime is unreadable (1.3:1). In the light theme the app switches to the olive step #588300, with white text.
  • The mark on white, green: G-W1 (a thin ink outline around the bright node). Bright lime alone disappears on white (1.3:1); the olive step (G-W2) is the "too dark" green you rejected.
  • Between the two: pink is the easier system. One magenta works on white and on dark, in the logo and in the app. Green needs three values (bright, graphics on white, text on white) and an outline on white, but it is the more distinctive color.

Where bright lime fails, and what the mark does there. On white the bright node measures 1.3:1, far under the 3:1 a graphic needs. Four answers (G-W4 is the one that stays under the guideline):

G-W1Bright node, thin ink outline

The node keeps its bright lime and gets a 1.5-unit outline in the ring ink, which carries the contrast. Bright everywhere, one green in the logo. At 16 px the outline and the lime blend into a gray-green dot.

G-W2Darker step on white

On white the node uses lime-500 #75a300 (exactly 3:1); on dark it is bright lime. This is close to the round 6 green you found too dark.

G-W4Round 2's lime on white, no outline

On white the node is round 2's #8fbf00, the green you first liked; on dark it is bright lime. It measures 2.18:1 on white: logos are exempt from WCAG's contrast rules, but this is under the 3:1 guideline for graphics, so the node is weaker than the rings on white and fades on a pale tab or page. Lighter and brighter than G-W2, and every child brightens with it.

G-W3Bright lime only on dark

On white, use the app icon (the dark rounded tile) instead of the open mark, so the lime always sits on dark. Simple, but every white placement becomes a tile.

2. How Figma carries its accent into the product

  • Figma swaps the interface accent per product. Its plugin color tokens define one "brand" token for the accent, described as "Our default accent color, which may shift between blue in Figma design and purple in FigJam" (Figma plugin docs, CSS variables and theming). So the product's accent is a single swappable token, and each product sets it to its own color. That is the pattern you saw in Figma Buzz. I could not find a public page that names Buzz's own accent hex, so I am not quoting one.
  • graphty's app is already built this way. Its theme (compact-mantine, modeled on Figma's tokens) has one accent token family (bg-brand, text-brand, border-selected and the selection tints), all driven by one primary color. The screens below change only that one color.
  • Brand color versus UI primary. Design-system guidance separates the two: a brand palette says what the colors are, and a UI color system says which shade goes where (Atmos, building a UI color system). The brand color should be checked at the tones the interface actually uses, not only the marketing version, and reserved for the places you want attention rather than used everywhere (same source; Material Design's color system pairs a primary and secondary with "on" colors for text placed on them). That is why GR-B pairs lime with dark text, and why PK-B and PK-C exist as calmer alternatives.

3. The real graphty app in each accent

The real app (the new workspace), built on a scratch branch that changes only the theme's primary color, with Les Miserables open and Louvain run. Dark theme first (the app's default), then light. Click any screen for full size. The recommended pair (PK-A and GR-B) is outlined.

Where the accent becomes a problem.

  • GR-A, raw lime: illegible. Every filled button (Run, Done) and the active toolbar tool put white text or icons on lime: 1.3:1. In the light theme the lime links on white are unreadable too. Lime cannot be the accent without dark text on it, which is what GR-B does.
  • PK-A, magenta: works. White on magenta is 4.5:1, links on the dark panel use the lighter #f06592 (4.6:1). The accent touches few surfaces (filled buttons, the active tool, links, the search field's focus edge), so it never floods the screen. One catch, shared by every accent: the graph's own community colors include a pink and a green, so a magenta or lime selection can look like "another group" on a colored graph. Selection outlines on the canvas should not rely on the accent alone.
  • PK-B, berry: calmer and darker; the buttons look heavier on the dark theme, and the links stay the same light pink. PK-C and GR-C move the app to indigo or teal and keep the brand color for the logo only: calm and safe, but the app no longer feels like the logo.
  • GR-D (light theme only): GR-B with round 2's #8fbf00 for graphics (icons, selected and focus edges) and #588300 for text and buttons. Brighter and closer to the logo than GR-B's all-olive light theme; the lime edges measure 2.18:1 on white, under the 3:1 guideline for focus indicators, so focus rings are harder to see.
  • Focus rings use the same token (border-selected), so they follow each accent: magenta and lime rings are easy to see on dark; on white, lime-700 is needed (GR-B does this).
