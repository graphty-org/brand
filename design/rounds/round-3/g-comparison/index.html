The three "g" marks side by side, and a best-of proposal

All four are the same idea: a two-storey lowercase g drawn as a graph. The top bowl and the bottom loop are ring nodes joined by an edge, and the ear is an edge out to an amber node. They differ only in sizes and positions. Each strip: 512 (shown at 192), 128, 32, 16, then 16 and 32 magnified 4x and 2x so the pixels show. Rows: light, dark, one color. Colors are indigo and amber in every column so only the shape changes.

The best-of g, one choice per feature

  1. Ring sizes from 2A. Bowl and loop are the same size (26 outer with a 12-unit hole each), so the mark reads as two equal nodes of a graph first and a letter second, and neither hole shrinks to a single pixel at 16 px the way round 1 A's loop does.
  2. Left-swinging stem from 2A, but steadier. The loop sits 8 units left of the bowl instead of 11: still a clear zigzag that can never be an 8 (2G's flaw), but less of a lean, so the g stands up straight.
  3. Long visible edge from 2G. The rings are pulled apart until about 5 units of edge show between them, so at 16 px there is a real line between two nodes, not two touching rings (2A's flaw at small sizes).
  4. Ear from round 1 A. The ear edge rises steeply toward the top-right corner (about 23 degrees: between round 1 A's 31 and 2G's flat 15, the steepest that still fits the square) and shows about 5 units of edge, so it reads as an edge leaving the letter, and the g keeps its ear rather than a flag.
  5. Stroke and amber from 2A and 2G, amber slightly bigger. Every edge is 6 thick (round 1 A's thinner 5-unit ear renders lighter than the rings at 16 px), rings stay 7, and the amber node grows from 11 to 12 across so the focus node still holds at 16 px.
