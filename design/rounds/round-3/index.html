4. Palettes

What the color research says, briefly
  • Most color psychology is weak: effects depend on context and replicate poorly (Elliot and Maier 2014). The popular "62-90% of judgments are color" figure is not an experimental result.
  • Lightness and saturation matter more than hue (Valdez and Mehrabian 1994; Gorn et al. 1997). So every palette here has the same structure: a dark, muted primary for credibility and one saturated focus node for energy.
  • In logos, blue reads as competent and red as exciting (Labrecque and Milne 2012); color-emotion links are largely shared across 30 nations (Jonauskaite et al. 2020), which matters for an international research audience.
  • People dislike olive and brown (Palmer and Schloss 2010). That bites us: lime or amber darkened enough to show on white turns olive (#75a300) or brown (#b36200).
  • Scientists judge color by whether color-blind readers can use it (Crameri et al. 2020; about 8% of men have a red-green deficiency). Blue against yellow is the safe axis, and at 16 px only a lightness difference between the node and the strokes survives.
  • Taken in our field: orange (Cytoscape #EA9123), blue (Neo4j, Linkurious), red (igraph, sigma.js), teal (Graphistry), lilac (KeyLines). Free: magenta, lime, plum, deep green. Two clashes: amber next to Cytoscape's logo in the co-brand, and lime on the webgpu chip reads a little like NVIDIA.

Full notes with citations: color/notes.md; every contrast number: color/contrast.json.

Shown on the 3B mark with round 2's placeholder wordmark, so only the color changes. Where the designed focus color fails 3:1 on white, the light mark uses a darker variant (listed in the notes).

P1Ink + lime #2a3045 / #8fbf00

Strongest overall. The node keeps its color under every color-vision type (lightness ratio about 4.3 to the strokes). Most distinctive in the field, clear of Cytoscape. Costs: the lime has to darken to #75a300 (olive) on white, and the near-black is severe.

P2Graphite + magenta #2e323c / #e0306f

The only accent that passes as text on both white and near-black without a darker variant; unclaimed among graph tools. For red-green color-blind readers the node loses its color and keeps only a lightness gap (2.11 under protanopia).

P3Indigo + amber #4f46e5 / #f5a000

Warm, color-blind safe (blue/yellow), and indigo matches graphty-element's default node color. But amber merges with Cytoscape's orange in the co-brand, the pair is close to Vite's identity, and amber goes brown on white.

P4Cobalt + lime (new) #1f45c8 / #9ad300

P1 with a friendlier blue in place of near-black. Best dark-mode separation of the six. The strongest new option.

P5Spruce + sun (new) #0e5146 / #ffbf00

Deep green speaks to life-science readers; color-blind safe. The yellow goes mustard on white and sits near Cytoscape's orange.

P6Aubergine + mint (new) #4a1f5c / #14c08e

Plum is the least-claimed primary. Mint fades toward gray in dark mode for deuteranopes. Worth a look, probably not the winner.

Color-vision check: each palette as people with deuteranopia and protanopia see it, with the contrast ratios.

5. Font study

Ten open-licensed sans serifs, all from Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, chosen toward your "simple sans like Amazon Ember" lean. The two fonts from round 2 are shown for reference only: Manrope (2A) and Space Grotesk (2B, 2E, the sharp-cornered g and y you disliked). Each row sets "graphty" next to the mark on light and dark, the package name in the same font and in JetBrains Mono, and a close-up of the g and y.

Every g and y side by side
F1Figtree (top 3)

Closest open font to Amazon Ember: single-story g with a round open tail, soft terminals, plain humanist shapes. Its lowercase lines up with the mark's rings; crisp at 12 px.

F2Nunito Sans (top 3)

Warmest and most readable, rounded joins, curved g tail. Risk: a little generic, so the mark carries the identity.

F3Plus Jakarta Sans (top 3)

Most polished: tall lowercase, compact, single-story g. More "modern startup" than Ember; body text sets tight.

F4DM Sans

Bold g is two-story, which competes with the mark (itself a two-story g).

F5Source Sans 3

Bold g is two-story; same problem as F4.

F6Inter

Excellent text face, but no identity of its own as a logo.

F7Onest

Its y has the squared u-shape that made Space Grotesk read as 1970s sci-fi.

F8Outfit

Wide and cold beside the mark.

F9Rubik

Playful and heavy.

F10Lexend

Makes a long, flat lockup.
