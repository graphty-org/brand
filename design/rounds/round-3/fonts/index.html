Font study for the graphty wordmark

Each row sets "graphty" in one open-licensed sans serif (bold, 700) beside the round 2 option 2A mark, then the package-name pairing two ways: the same family at regular weight in indigo, and JetBrains Mono. The strip below and each row's right column blow up the g and the y, the two letters the round 2 fonts got wrong. Bottom rows are the two rejected round 2 fonts, for reference. All fonts are from Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.

What makes Amazon Ember work, and what each row is judged on: humanist proportions (letters of differing widths, not all circles), open apertures, a single-story g, soft or rounded terminals, a generous x-height, and a y drawn as a plain v with a tail, not the squared u-shape of Space Grotesk and Onest.

1. Figtree
Closest to Ember: single-story g with a round open tail, soft terminals, plain humanist proportions. Its x-height lines up with the mark's two rings, so the word and the mark read as one unit. Stays crisp at 12 px. Pair it with Figtree Regular for package names.
2. Nunito Sans
The warmest of the set: rounded joins, open apertures, a single-story g with a full curved tail. Very readable at nav and body sizes. The risk is that it feels a little generic; the mark has to carry the identity.
3. Plus Jakarta Sans
The most polished: tall x-height, compact width, single-story g. Reads more "modern startup" than Ember, and its body text sets tight at small sizes, but the wordmark itself is the sharpest-looking of the three without being sharp-cornered.

Package-name pairing: the same family at Regular in the accent color reads as one brand and is the recommendation for logos and lockups. JetBrains Mono (SIL OFL 1.1) says "npm package" clearly and suits docs, README headers and code contexts; it is the alternative, not the default.
